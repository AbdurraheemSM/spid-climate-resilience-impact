# SUSTAINABLE PARTICIPATORY DEVELOPMENT AND CLIMATE RESILIENCE INITIATIVE (SPiD)

## Research Brief: Promoting Community Participation and Climate Resilience in Nigeria

### 1. Introduction: Who Are They?

The Sustainable Participatory Development and Climate Resilience Initiative (SPiD) is a Nigerian non-profit organisation based in Kaduna State.

SPiD promotes participatory climate and environmental sustainability policies, governance, and programme development. The organisation works to ensure that communities, young people, and institutions have opportunities to contribute to decisions affecting their environment and sustainable development.

Its approach focuses on community participation, capacity development, climate governance, research, and inclusive development.

SPiD aims to empower underserved communities and young people to become active contributors and leaders in sustainable development.

### 2. What Data and Information Do They Use?

SPiD uses community experiences, local knowledge, research findings, and stories to understand environmental challenges and improve climate-related decision-making.

Important information areas include:

- Community experiences of climate change.
- Local environmental challenges and vulnerabilities.
- Household experiences and community needs.
- Climate adaptation and mitigation practices.
- Community participation in environmental governance.
- The experiences of young people and underserved groups.
- Information about sustainable agriculture, renewable energy, and water management.

These sources help the organisation understand what communities need and develop more inclusive approaches to climate action.

### 3. How Does SPiD Use Community Participation?

Community participation is central to SPiD's approach.

Rather than treating communities as passive recipients of development programmes, SPiD promotes their involvement in identifying challenges, sharing experiences, contributing ideas, and influencing decisions.

Its approach includes:

1. Engaging community members in discussions about environmental challenges.
2. Collecting local experiences and information.
3. Encouraging young people to participate in climate leadership.
4. Strengthening the capacity of community organisations.
5. Connecting community perspectives with policymakers and institutions.

This approach helps ensure that climate programmes reflect the actual needs and priorities of the people they are intended to serve.

### 4. What Has Changed Because of Their Work?

One important initiative associated with SPiD is the Community-led Participatory Initiative for Climate Governance (CPI4CG).

The initiative focuses on understanding how communities experience climate change and participate in environmental decision-making.

SPiD has also highlighted research from Sabon Gari Local Government Area in Kaduna State through its report, *Voices from the Ward*.

The research examines climate vulnerability, awareness, adaptation, and governance across the area's eleven wards.

A particularly important finding shared by SPiD is that 99% of surveyed respondents expressed willingness to participate in climate initiatives, while only 19% had attended a climate or environmental meeting during the previous year.

This finding highlights an important gap between people's willingness to participate and their actual involvement in climate governance.

It suggests that communities may be interested in climate action but need more accessible opportunities to contribute to environmental decisions.

### 5. Why Is This Work Important for Nigeria?

Nigeria faces several environmental and climate-related challenges, including flooding, irregular rainfall, soil erosion, deforestation, and pressure on agricultural livelihoods.

These challenges affect communities differently, making local knowledge and participation important in developing effective responses.

SPiD's approach is relevant because it:

- Encourages inclusive climate decision-making.
- Promotes youth participation and leadership.
- Supports community-based climate research.
- Strengthens environmental awareness.
- Encourages collaboration between communities and institutions.
- Promotes sustainable development and climate resilience.

By bringing community experiences into climate governance, SPiD helps connect environmental policies with the realities of everyday life.

### 6. Challenges and Opportunities

Some challenges that can affect participatory climate initiatives include:

- Limited awareness of climate policies.
- Inadequate opportunities for community participation.
- Limited access to reliable environmental information.
- Insufficient funding for grassroots programmes.
- Communication barriers between communities and policymakers.
- Limited representation of underserved groups in decision-making.

However, these challenges also create opportunities for stronger community engagement.

SPiD can continue strengthening its work through accessible climate education, youth leadership programmes, community research, partnerships, and improved communication between local communities and government institutions.

### 7. Recommendations

Based on the organisation's participatory approach, the following recommendations could strengthen climate resilience in Nigeria:

1. Expand community climate education programmes, especially in underserved areas.
2. Create more opportunities for young people to participate in environmental governance.
3. Support women and other underserved groups to contribute to climate decisions.
4. Strengthen collaboration between civil society organisations, communities, and government institutions.
5. Use research and local knowledge to develop practical climate solutions.
6. Improve the collection and use of community-level environmental data.
7. Establish regular community dialogue sessions to monitor environmental challenges and evaluate progress.

### 8. Conclusion

The Sustainable Participatory Development and Climate Resilience Initiative demonstrates the importance of including communities in climate action and sustainable development.

Its work highlights that effective climate governance requires more than policies and programmes. It also requires listening to communities, understanding their experiences, strengthening their capacity, and giving them meaningful opportunities to participate.

By promoting participatory development, climate research, and youth leadership, SPiD contributes to the broader goal of building more inclusive and climate-resilient communities in Nigeria.
